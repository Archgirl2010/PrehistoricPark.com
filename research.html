Research at Prehistoric Park

Studying
the Past.

Our research teams work alongside animal care specialists to improve our understanding of prehistoric species and the environments in which they once lived.

Research Programme

Science in a Living Environment

Prehistoric Park provides researchers with an unprecedented opportunity to study living representatives of species otherwise known only through fossils and scientific reconstruction.

Research is conducted throughout the park in partnership with our zoological, veterinary and animal care teams. Observations are designed to improve our understanding of animal behaviour, biology, ecology and welfare.

Much of our research is conducted without disrupting normal animal behaviour. Long-term observation, environmental monitoring and behavioural recording allow researchers to build detailed profiles of individual animals and populations.

Behaviour & Ecology

Researchers study social behaviour, communication, feeding patterns, territorial behaviour and interactions between individuals.

Genetics

Genetic research supports population management and helps maintain healthy and diverse breeding populations across the park.

Animal Biology

Physiological research helps our teams understand growth, development, metabolism and other aspects of prehistoric animal biology.

Habitat Science

Environmental data from across the park is used to refine habitat design, climate control, vegetation and enrichment programmes.

Research Facility Notice

Research facilities and animal care areas are restricted to authorised personnel. Visitors may observe selected research activities from designated public viewing areas.

A Living Archive

Understanding What Came Before

Fossils tell us what prehistoric animals looked like. Living animals can tell us how they behaved.

By combining modern zoological science with palaeontological research, Prehistoric Park aims to develop a deeper understanding of the world's prehistoric ecosystems and the animals that once inhabited them.
