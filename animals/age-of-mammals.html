After the Dinosaurs

The Age of Mammals explores a very different chapter of prehistoric life. Its inhabitants come from several different periods of Earth's history, representing the extraordinary diversity of mammals that evolved long after the dinosaurs had disappeared.

The habitat combines open grassland, woodland and dry scrubland. Large animals have access to broad spaces while smaller species can retreat into more sheltered areas.

Some Things Look Surprisingly Familiar
Kangaroos, rhinoceroses and other modern-looking animals have ancient relatives represented here — although some members of this collection were considerably larger than anything alive today.

Visitors follow a wide walking trail around the habitat, with several elevated viewing points overlooking the grasslands and woodland.

The Residents

Paraceratherium

Paraceratherium

One of the largest land mammals ever known. This enormous hornless rhinoceros relative towers above the surrounding vegetation.

Diprotodon

Diprotodon optatum

Australia's giant marsupial herbivore. Diprotodon resembles an enormous wombat and is one of the park's most distinctive mammals.

Andrewsarchus

Andrewsarchus mongoliensis

A huge prehistoric mammal known primarily from its enormous skull. Its exact lifestyle remains the subject of scientific discussion.

Archaeotherium

Archaeotherium

An ancient entelodont with a powerful build and formidable jaws. Despite its pig-like appearance, it was a very different animal from modern pigs.

Procoptodon

Procoptodon goliah

The largest known short-faced kangaroo. Procoptodon stood far taller than modern kangaroos and inhabited Ice Age Australia.

Megatherium

Megatherium americanum

A gigantic ground sloth that could rear up on its hind legs to reach vegetation well above the ground.

The Age of Mammals Experience

🌾 Open Range

Large grassland areas allow the biggest mammals to move naturally across the habitat.

🌳 Mammal Woodland

Follow the trail through sheltered woodland where several species spend time feeding and resting.

🔭 Giant Mammal Lookout

Elevated viewing points provide a clear view across the main grassland and its larger residents.

Visitor Safety The Age of Mammals contains large free-moving animals. Visitors must remain on designated paths and behind protective barriers. Do not attempt to feed, touch or attract the animals.
