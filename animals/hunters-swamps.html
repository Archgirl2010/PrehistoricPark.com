Welcome to Blackwater Bog

Hunters Swamps is one of the most unusual habitats in Prehistoric Park. A network of deep pools, slow-moving channels, mud banks and dense prehistoric vegetation creates an environment designed for animals that spend much of their lives in or around water.

The main visitor route is an elevated boardwalk that crosses several sections of the swamp. Viewing platforms provide sightlines across the water while keeping guests well above the habitat.

Watch the Water
Not everything in Hunters Swamps is visible at first glance. Some residents spend long periods partially submerged or hidden among the vegetation.

The swamp is deliberately maintained as a complex, naturalistic environment rather than a simple pool. Shallow wetlands, deep water and dry islands allow the different species to occupy separate areas of the habitat.

The Residents

Sarcosuchus

Giant Crocodyliform

One of the largest crocodile-like predators in the park. Sarcosuchus spends much of its time close to the water and can remain remarkably still for long periods.

Kaprosuchus

Crocodyliform

Known for its prominent teeth and unusual skull, Kaprosuchus is primarily observed around the muddy margins and shallow sections of the habitat.

Purussaurus

Giant Caiman

An enormous prehistoric caiman capable of delivering an exceptionally powerful bite. Deep water provides the ideal environment for this massive predator.

Stomatosuchus

Crocodyliform

A specialised ancient crocodile relative with a distinctive broad skull. It occupies the quieter waterways of the swamp.

Carnufex

Terrestrial Predator

Unlike the aquatic hunters, Carnufex spends most of its time on land. It occupies the raised forested islands and perimeter areas of the habitat.

The Blackwater Bog Experience

🌿 Swamp Boardwalk

Follow the elevated timber boardwalk through dense vegetation and across the main waterways.

🐊 Predator Pools

Protected viewing platforms overlook several deep pools where the largest aquatic predators spend much of their time.

🌫 Blackwater Lookout

A raised observation point provides a broad view across the central swamp and its surrounding forest.

Visitor Safety The waterways contain large aquatic predators. Visitors must remain on designated boardwalks and viewing platforms. Do not lean over barriers, throw objects into the water or attempt to feed the animals.
