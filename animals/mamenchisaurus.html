ABOUT THE SPECIES

MAMENCHISAURUS

Mamenchisaurus was a gigantic long-necked sauropod dinosaur that lived during the Late Jurassic Period, approximately 160 to 145 million years ago.

It is particularly famous for its extraordinary neck, which could account for roughly half of its total body length. Its long neck allowed it to reach vegetation across a wide feeding area without constantly moving its enormous body.

HABITAT & RANGE

WHERE IT LIVED

Fossils of Mamenchisaurus have been discovered in what is now China, particularly in deposits that preserve ancient floodplains, forests and seasonal environments.

The species lived in landscapes containing abundant vegetation and water sources, supporting large populations of plant-eating dinosaurs.

DIET

WHAT IT ATE

Mamenchisaurus was a herbivore. Its long neck allowed it to reach vegetation at different heights, giving it access to leaves and plant material unavailable to many other herbivores.

Its diet likely consisted of leaves, shoots, ferns, branches and other vegetation available in its environment.

BEHAVIOUR

HOW IT LIVED

Mamenchisaurus was a large, slow-moving herbivore. Its enormous size would have provided protection from many predators, while its long neck allowed it to feed across a broad area.

Scientists believe large sauropods may have travelled in groups or loose herds, although the exact social behaviour of Mamenchisaurus remains uncertain.
