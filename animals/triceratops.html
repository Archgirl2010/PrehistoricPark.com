About the species

Triceratops

Triceratops was a large herbivorous dinosaur that lived during the Late Cretaceous Period, approximately 68 to 66 million years ago.

It is recognised by its enormous skull, three facial horns and large bony frill. Despite its imposing appearance, Triceratops was primarily a plant-eater.

Habitat & Range

Where It Lived

Triceratops fossils have been found across western North America, particularly in areas that once contained extensive floodplains and vegetation.

The animal likely lived in environments containing abundant low-growing vegetation, rivers and seasonal water sources.

Diet

What It Ate

Triceratops was a herbivore. Its powerful beak and rows of teeth were adapted for cropping and processing vegetation.

Its diet may have included ferns, shrubs, low-growing plants and other vegetation available in its environment.

Understanding the animal

Behaviour

Herd Behaviour

Triceratops may have gathered in groups, although the exact nature of these groups remains uncertain. Their large frills and horns may have played roles in communication or display.

Defence

The three horns and enormous skull would have provided an effective defensive structure against predators. Adults were formidable animals.

Feeding

Triceratops possessed a powerful beak suited to cropping vegetation. Its jaw mechanics allowed it to process tough plant material efficiently.

Visitor Notice

Visitors must remain behind designated barriers. Do not attempt to feed or approach the animals. Triceratops may display defensive behaviour when disturbed or approached unexpectedly.
