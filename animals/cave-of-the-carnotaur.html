Inside the Cave

The Cave of the Carnotaur is a purpose-built rocky habitat designed around the behaviour of a single Carnotaurus. The enclosure incorporates tunnels, chambers, elevated ledges and concealed holding areas.

Visitors follow a controlled path through the rock formation, entering a series of viewing chambers positioned around the habitat.

A Different Kind of Encounter
Unlike the open habitats elsewhere in Prehistoric Park, the Cave of the Carnotaur deliberately limits visibility. The animal may be visible in one chamber and completely absent from another.

Low lighting and naturalistic rock formations allow the Carnotaurus to retreat from view when it chooses. Visitors are encouraged to remain quiet while inside the viewing tunnels.

The Carnotaur

Carnotaurus

Carnotaurus sastrei
Period: Late Cretaceous
Location: Patagonia
Diet: Carnivorous
Feature: Prominent horns
Habitat: Rocky woodland

Carnotaurus was a lightly built but powerful theropod distinguished by the pair of prominent horns above its eyes. Its unusually short arms and deep skull give it a distinctive appearance among the park's predators.

The Cave Experience

🪨 Rock Tunnels

Walk through a network of artificial limestone and basalt formations surrounding the main habitat.

👁 Viewing Chambers

Multiple protected viewing areas provide different perspectives into the enclosure.

🌑 Low-Light Habitat

The darker environment allows the Carnotaurus to move between visible and concealed areas naturally.

Visitor Safety This is a predator habitat. Visitors must remain behind designated barriers and follow staff instructions at all times. Do not knock on viewing glass, shout into the habitat, use flash photography or attempt to attract the animal.
