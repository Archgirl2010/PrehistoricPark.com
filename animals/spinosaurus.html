About the species

Spinosaurus

Spinosaurus aegyptiacus was a large theropod dinosaur that lived during the Late Cretaceous Period, approximately 100 to 94 million years ago.

It is recognised by its elongated skull, conical teeth and distinctive neural spines that formed a large sail-like structure along its back.

Spinosaurus is particularly notable for its adaptations to a life closely associated with water, making it one of the most unusual large predatory dinosaurs known.

Habitat & Range

Where It Lived

Fossil evidence indicates that Spinosaurus inhabited parts of northern Africa during the Late Cretaceous.

Its environment included extensive river systems, wetlands, coastal areas and other aquatic habitats. These environments supported abundant fish populations.

Diet

What It Ate

Spinosaurus appears to have been strongly adapted for catching fish. Its long narrow jaws and conical teeth were well suited to gripping slippery aquatic prey.

Fossil evidence suggests that fish formed an important component of its diet, although it may also have taken other available prey.

Behaviour

How It Lived

Spinosaurus appears to have spent substantial amounts of time around water. Its anatomy suggests a lifestyle significantly different from that of terrestrial predators such as Tyrannosaurus.

Exactly how much time Spinosaurus spent swimming remains an active area of scientific research.

Understanding the animal

Behaviour

Aquatic Feeding

Its elongated jaws and conical teeth were well suited to catching fish. The animal may have used its jaws to seize prey in shallow water.

The Sail

The tall neural spines along the back formed a prominent structure. Scientists continue to debate whether this feature was primarily used for display, thermoregulation or other functions.

Water Dependence

Spinosaurus shows a collection of anatomical adaptations associated with aquatic environments, making it one of the most specialised large theropods known.

Visitor Notice

Blackwater Lagoon is a restricted viewing area. Visitors must remain within designated observation facilities. Do not approach the waterline or attempt to enter the lagoon under any circumstances.
