About the species

Velociraptor

Velociraptor mongoliensis was a relatively small dromaeosaurid dinosaur that lived during the Late Cretaceous Period, approximately 75 to 71 million years ago.

Despite its relatively small size, Velociraptor was an active predator. It possessed a long tail, a narrow skull and a large curved claw on the second toe of each foot.

Fossil evidence also indicates that Velociraptor possessed feathers, although it could not fly.

Habitat & Range

Where It Lived

Velociraptor fossils are known primarily from what is now Mongolia and surrounding regions of Central Asia.

Its environment included arid landscapes, sand dunes, temporary waterways and areas of vegetation.

Diet

What It Ate

Velociraptor was a carnivore. Its diet likely included small animals and reptiles, although larger prey may occasionally have been targeted.

One famous fossil preserves a Velociraptor apparently engaged in combat with a Protoceratops, providing rare evidence of predator-prey interaction.

Behaviour

How It Lived

Velociraptor was likely an agile and active predator. Its long hind limbs and stiffened tail would have provided balance while moving quickly across its environment.

The extent to which Velociraptor hunted cooperatively in groups remains uncertain. Popular depictions of coordinated pack hunting go beyond what the fossil evidence can currently establish.

Understanding the animal

Behaviour

Hunting

Velociraptor possessed a large curved toe claw that was likely used during attacks or to restrain prey.

Movement

Its long hind limbs and balancing tail indicate an animal adapted for active terrestrial movement.

Feathers

Fossil evidence shows that Velociraptor had feathers. These were primarily associated with insulation, display or other functions rather than powered flight.
