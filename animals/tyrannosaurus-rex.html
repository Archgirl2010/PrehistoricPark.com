About the species

Tyrannosaurus Rex

Tyrannosaurus rex was one of the largest known terrestrial predators. It lived during the Late Cretaceous Period, approximately 68 to 66 million years ago, in what is now western North America.

T. rex possessed a massive skull, powerful jaws and large, robust teeth adapted for cutting and crushing flesh and bone.

Habitat & Range

Where It Lived

Fossils of Tyrannosaurus rex have been discovered across western North America, particularly in deposits that preserve ancient floodplains, forests and coastal environments.

The environments inhabited by T. rex were very different from the landscapes found in the region today.

Diet

What It Ate

Tyrannosaurus rex was a carnivore. Evidence from fossilised bite marks and tooth structure indicates that it was capable of consuming large prey and processing substantial amounts of flesh and bone.

Understanding the animal

Behaviour

Hunting

T. rex was a powerful predator capable of delivering extremely strong bites. Scientists continue to investigate whether individuals actively hunted prey, scavenged, or used a combination of both strategies.

Social Behaviour

Evidence concerning social behaviour in Tyrannosaurus remains limited. Fossil sites containing multiple individuals have generated debate about whether the animals sometimes tolerated or interacted with one another.

Senses

Tyrannosaurus possessed large forward-facing eyes and sensory adaptations consistent with an active predatory lifestyle.

Visitor Notice

Visitors are required to remain behind designated safety barriers while viewing Tyrannosaurus rex. Do not attempt to attract the attention of the animals.
